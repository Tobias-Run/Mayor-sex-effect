# Literature and design crosswalk

Completed targeted review, **5 October 2026 (FP02, version 1)**. **This project adapts Florio and Spagnolo (2026), [*Female Mayors and Public Procurement*](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7046899), to Germany.** German foundations include Schild (2013), **Baskaran and Hessami (2018)** on female mayors in Hesse, Baskaran and Hessami (2025) on Bavarian councillors, Arnold (2018), Frank, Stadelmann and Torgler (2023), and GERDA. The 2018 mayoral study is an additional direct German design precedent; it must not be conflated with the council study.

The deliverables are this methods/outcomes comparison, a [primary-source manifest](literature-source-manifest.csv), a [dated search log](research-continuation-search-log.csv) and [response inventory](research-continuation-search-responses.csv). FP02 completes the bounded comparison of these foundations, with the version and access limits below. It does not complete a systematic review, establish novelty, reproduce published estimates or authorize main effects. The [outcome/population codebook draft](outcome-population-codebook.md) translates the comparison into construction rules; final coverage, windows and confirmatory outcomes remain separate gates.

## Decisions informed by the comparison

1. **Keep gender provenance and the estimand honest.** Italy, Schild and the Hesse working paper describe administrative candidate gender. Our current measure is historically linked public presentation, with administrative fields unavailable. GERDA predictions and names do not fill that gap. The local comparison elects the female-presented candidate instead of her actual male-presented opponent; it does not isolate gender while holding party, experience or all other attributes constant.
2. **Use exact decisive margins and state rules.** A 51%–49% exhaustive two-person race has a 2 pp candidate-difference margin, while the female share is only 1 pp above 50%. Numerical bandwidths expressed in those two scales are not interchangeable. The council paper divides by the two marginal candidates' combined preferential votes; that is not a municipal valid-vote denominator. The existing NRW codebook and exact vote files govern our margin.
3. **Define weights explicitly.** Italy Section 4 uses contract-level regressions with mayor-level clustered standard errors. The council working paper, p. 16, weights duplicated municipality-year/race observations by the inverse number of observations from that municipality-year, and clusters by municipality. That is a concrete precedent for preventing repeated records from increasing a municipality's weight. Our draft prefers one election-level outcome row; a pooled result-weighted analysis addresses a different target.
4. **Retain election assignment through turnover for the proposed fixed-window ITT.** Italy assigns procedures published under the winner and separately restricts publication/conclusion to the same mayor (Section 5.2, pp. 16–17; Tables A.4–A.5). Its delayed-publication check uses at least six months after the election (Section 5.3, p. 18; A.6). Those exposure restrictions differ from keeping the original election assignment in a fixed calendar window. They belong in declared sensitivity populations, with selection and attrition reported.
5. **Separate procedure composition from tender competition.** Italy Table 2 includes direct awards, whereas Table 3's bidder population is Open&Negotiated. German `neg-wo-call` records require their own category and timing; they are not automatically Italian direct awards. Procedure choice can respond to the election. A tender mean conditional on realized procedure, award and reported count is consequently not the unconditional effect on all municipal purchases.
6. **Audit reporting before interpreting outcomes.** Italy Section 3.1, pp. 7–8, reports about 49% phase-complete contracts and about 76% missing adjudication for direct awards. Its missingness tests do not validate German completeness. The council paper's candidate coverage declines from 76.9% in 2014 to 49.1% in 2008 and 28.3% in 2002; included municipalities are larger (Section 3.1.1; A.4). Our published attrition must retain unknown candidate evidence, unlinked phases, missing counts and reporting strata.
7. **Use contemporary local-linear inference as the proposed primary design.** Italy reports an optimal-bandwidth specification and cubic alternatives; the Hesse working paper uses CCT bandwidths, local linear/quadratic models and municipal clustering. Do not inherit cubic polynomials, numeric bandwidths or contract-level precision. FP15 must assess discrete margins, distinct municipalities, dependence, useful precision and small-cluster limitations before the main estimator is frozen.
8. **Do not inherit potentially post-election controls.** Italy's tables include contract category/size and procedure indicators in some regressions. Those variables may themselves respond to treatment. Our primary adjustment candidates must be dated before the 2020 election; conditional mechanism analyses need a separate interpretation.

The review by Hessami and Lopes da Fonseca (2020), Sections 3–5, helps place fiscal, specific-policy and political-representation findings in context. It does not establish that women should improve every procurement measure. No foreign effect direction or magnitude is used as an expected German result or as a precision input without justification.

## Replication routes and unfinished version checks

The **2018 German mayoral study has a public [openICPSR V1 replication record](https://www.openicpsr.org/openicpsr/project/114710/version/V1/view), [doi:10.3886/E114710V1](https://doi.org/10.3886/E114710V1)**, dated 13 October 2019. The original landing page was acquired and pins its project identity, version, data folder and license file. Search-indexed folder listings additionally expose readme and figure/table scripts. **The package, license text and data have not been downloaded or audited.** A replication record does not establish present download rights, compatible fields, NRW coverage or permission to republish historical candidate data. Inspect the readme/license and dated Hesse mayoral sources in a bounded follow-up before considering reuse; no contact with the authors is needed to inspect public materials.

For Italy, Schild, the 2025 council article and Arnold, no new executable replication package was verified in this block. This is an access finding, not an assertion that none exists. The Hesse journal PDF and final council text remain version-check tasks; their accessible older primary editions support the qualified methods comparison here. Frank's published availability statement offers data on request; sending a request remains outside the current authorization.

## Search scope and contribution statement

The recorded search comprises **15 queries in four batches: six candidate-source queries and nine literature/data queries**, plus four navigation batches. Literature searches used English and German combinations of female mayors, Germany, public procurement/Vergabe, the named German studies, replication and GERDA. Papers are included here when they directly inform the Italian procurement adaptation, German mayoral/council identification, source acquisition or outcome measurement. Search results are discovery leads; acquisition and primary inspection support the matrix.

The searches located the additional Hesse study and the broader 2020 review, and **did not identify an additional verified German female-mayor procurement-effect paper in this bounded round**. The returned international procurement/corruption leads are not proof of a German study; generic procurement reports and qualitative career studies address other questions. Batched search output is not an exhaustive per-query census. A correction and later editions remain unverified as stated above.

Use the provisional contribution statement: **“We adapt the Italian close-election procurement design to Germany, building on German evidence about female mayors and councillors, and assess whether linked public records support comparable procurement outcomes and documented strategic-criteria extensions.”** Do not claim “the first German study” or an absence of previous German research. Update this comparison if a new primary study or final-edition difference changes an adaptation decision.
